# v0.8.3 — player transport: a re-armable guest handshake

**Branch:** `v0.8.3/player-transport` · **Closes:** #213, #211 · **Status:** spec, rev 2 (post-review)

**Research input:** `docs/research/2026-08-02-webview-messageport-handshake.md`.
**Review:** rev 1 was reviewed and returned **RED** with 10 blockers. Rev 2 resolves all of them; §14
records what changed and why, so the plan reviewer can check the resolutions rather than re-deriving
them. Where this spec and the research doc disagree, this spec wins (§13).

## 1. Problem

The docked YouTube player talks to its guest page over a `MessagePort` RPC channel. Two defects,
filed separately, are the same omission.

**#213 — the channel is established once and never re-established.** `onDomReady`
(`playerSingleton.ts:174-205`) opens with `if (rpc || !wv) return` (`:176`), then assigns
`rpc = createRpc(port1)` at `:179` — *before* the guest runtime is injected (`:190`) and before the
port is transferred (`:192`). Two failures follow:

- **Narrow (as filed):** the guest swaps documents between `:190` and `:192`; the port is orphaned,
  `rpc` is non-null, every later `dom-ready` is refused.
- **Wide (dominant, not in the issue):** `dom-ready` fires once per committed main-frame document. If
  the *first* document is a consent wall or a redirect, the runtime is injected there and `rpc`
  latches to it. The real watch page's `dom-ready` is then refused **in the steady state**, not in a
  race. `scripts/thread-smoke.mjs:841-846` already documents this mechanism.

The symptom is deceptive: `play()` bypasses RPC by design (`:309-319`), and `safeInsertCSS()` sits
*outside* the guard (`:258`) and re-fires on every `dom-ready`, so the chrome hides correctly in
precisely the runs where the RPC is dead. Nothing logs an error.

**Failure rate — #213's body is self-contradictory and this spec does not adopt either figure.** It
says both "roughly 1 run in 7" (~14%) and "observed in 7 of 8 `SMOKE_PLAYBACK=1` runs" (~88%). These
cannot both be true. This spec's design does not depend on the rate — the mechanism is established
from the Electron C++ and from `thread-smoke.mjs:841-846`, not from the frequency. §11 corrects the
issue body; §8.3's gate replaces the statistical claim with a deterministic one.

**#211 — the value cache outlives the guest.** `load(id)` (`:299-307`) sets `videoId`, destroys
`rpc`, and reassigns `src` — but never touches `cache` (`:70-74`). Chain, each link verified:

1. `getDuration()` returns `cache.duration` (`:333-335`) — the *previous* video's, still non-null.
2. `usePlayer.ts` and `usePlayerState.ts` latch on the first non-null read (`durationDone`, declared
   at `:32` in **both** files; the polling block is `usePlayer.ts:38-45` / `usePlayerState.ts:40-47`)
   and never re-poll for the life of that mount.
3. `ThreadView.tsx:241-249` writes it: `api.videoSources.upsert(videoId, { durationSec: … })`,
   guarded by `durationWrittenRef`, once per mount.
4. `App.tsx` keys `ThreadView` by `threadNoteId`, so every thread open is a fresh mount with a fresh
   ref.

**Opening video B after video A persists A's duration onto B's `video_sources` row.** It is written
to disk and survives restart. `cache.last` is likewise never reset (§7 L1 covers the consequence).

## 2. Root cause

**Nothing invalidates player state when the guest document is replaced.** #213 is the RPC channel not
invalidated; #211 is the value cache not invalidated. One `teardown()`, called on the events that
mean "the current document is going away", addresses both. That is why they ship as one batch:
fixing them separately means writing the same invalidation twice and getting it subtly different.

## 3. Non-goals — settled, do not relitigate

**N1. Do NOT replace `contentWindow.postMessage`.** Undocumented and absent from `electron.d.ts` (we
declare it at `playerSingleton.ts:39`), but Electron 42.5.0 *defines* `contentWindow` in
`lib/renderer/web-view/web-view-impl.ts:49-55` and depends on it internally. **#213 is not a
wrong-API bug.**

**N2. Do NOT add a guest preload.** Closed by construction: `src/main/index.ts:276-282` does
`delete prefs.preload` + `nodeIntegration = false`, so there is no `ipcRenderer` in the guest to
receive a port. Taking this path reverses **ADR 0016 D7/D8**.

**N3. Do NOT route the port through main.** `webContents.postMessage` / `frame.postMessage` deliver
to `ipcRenderer` in the guest — same blocker as N2.

**N4. Do NOT change `play()`'s `executeJavaScript` gesture path** (`:309-319`).

**N5. Do NOT reveal the guest earlier than HEAD does.** The ~1s black cover deliberately masks
YouTube's startup churn; revealing early was tried at `47a05f7`, produced a "muted, plays 1s, then
stops" flash, and was reverted (`:199-203`, tracked in #65). **§5.6 is bound by this: the cover must
not drop on `ack`.**

**N6. Do NOT regress the consent-wall escape hatch.** At HEAD, `:197`'s 10s timeout drops the cover
even when the guest never becomes ready, so the user can see and dismiss a consent wall — which is
why the webview is left interactive (`:249-252`). Any redesign must keep a bounded unconditional
drop.

## 4. The contract

Recorded in words because v0.8.2's cluster B existed precisely because a prose-only contract rotted
invisibly for two milestones. §8 makes each clause executable.

> **C1.** `rpc` is non-null **iff** a channel has been acknowledged by the guest document that is
> **currently committed**. Never assigned before acknowledgement; never survives its document.
>
> **C2.** Every main-frame cross-document navigation invalidates all guest-derived channel and cache
> state. In-page (SPA) navigation does not. **Stated exception:** `cssKey` (`:67`) is guest-derived
> but is *not* reset — it self-heals on the next `dom-ready` and its failure path is caught at
> `:446-448`. C2 means "channel + value cache", not literally every module variable.
>
> **C3.** `ack` and `ready` are different facts. **`ack`** = transport is live; sent before any DOM
> work; fires even on a consent page. **`ready`** = a `<video>` is hooked. Neither may be inferred
> from the other. **The cover keys on `ready`-or-timeout, never on `ack`** (N5, N6).
>
> **C4.** An acknowledgement is honoured only if it echoes the token of the attempt that is still
> current. Late acks from superseded attempts or dead documents are dropped, never published.
>
> **C5.** Injecting the guest runtime twice into one document must not duplicate observers, intervals,
> rAF loops or listeners; the receiver must accept a second port without being re-injected.
>
> **C6.** The player never rests in a silent dead state. Every path that nulls `rpc` either reaches a
> published channel, or emits exactly one diagnostic carrying `webview.getURL()`, within a bounded
> time. **A navigation that starts and never commits is such a path** (§5.3).

## 5. Host-side design (`playerSingleton.ts`)

**5.1 Module state.**

```ts
let rpc: Rpc | null = null          // published: acked, current document (C1)
let pendingRpc: Rpc | null = null   // half-built, awaiting ack
let handshakeSeq = 0                // monotonic; identifies the current attempt (C4)
let attempts = 0                    // retries within the current document
let armWatchdog = 0                 // setTimeout handle; the C6 guarantee (§5.3)
let coverTimer = 0                  // bounded unconditional cover drop (N6)

// Test seam — mirrors createRpc's opts.invokeTimeoutMs (rpc.ts:22-23), which is the
// existing precedent for making a timing constant injectable.
export const handshakeConfig = { ackTimeoutMs: 3000, maxAttempts: 3, readyTimeoutMs: 10_000,
                                 armWatchdogMs: 4000 }
```

**5.2 `teardown()`** — the single point where C2 is enforced. In order:

1. `handshakeSeq++` — any in-flight ack is now stale by construction.
2. `attempts = 0`.
3. Destroy **and null** `pendingRpc`, then `rpc`. Because `teardown()` can run *between* §5.5 steps 1
   and 2, a candidate created afterwards would survive it — §5.5 step 2 therefore re-checks `seq`
   immediately after assigning `pendingRpc`, and destroys itself if superseded.
4. `resetCache()` → `{ currentTime: 0, duration: null, last: 'unstarted' }`.
5. Clear `coverTimer`; leave the cover in whatever state it is in (do not raise it here — `load()`
   raises it explicitly at `:304`).

`teardown()` does **not** arm the watchdog; §5.3 does, because only the navigation path needs it.

**5.3 Invalidation, and the paired re-arm guarantee (C6).**

Add a `did-start-navigation` listener; on `isMainFrame && !isInPlace`, call `teardown()` **and arm
`armWatchdog`** for `armWatchdogMs`.

**Why the watchdog is mandatory.** `did-start-navigation` and `dom-ready` are *not* a matched pair —
a navigation can start and never commit, and then no `dom-ready` ever arrives:

- Electron emits `did-start-navigation` unconditionally with no commit check, while its sibling
  `DidFinishNavigation` early-returns on `if (!navigation_handle->HasCommitted()) return;`
  (`shell/browser/api/electron_api_web_contents.cc` @ v42.5.0) — proving uncommitted navigations are
  a first-class case.
- Chromium's `content/public/browser/web_contents_observer.h`: *"There can be more than one
  navigation ongoing in the same frame at the same time … Each will get its own NavigationHandle."*
- Chromium `docs/navigation.md` lists 204/205 responses and `Content-Disposition` downloads as
  navigations that produce no new document.
- **Reachable in this repo:** `src/main/index.ts:288-297` cancels any guest navigation whose hostname
  misses `GUEST_HOST_ALLOW` (`:296` `event.preventDefault()`). YouTube ad click-throughs, description
  links and sign-in flows through non-allowlisted domains all hit it. BFCache restores too.

Without the watchdog, `teardown()` nulls `rpc`, nothing commits, no `dom-ready` fires, `handshake()`
is never entered, and C6's diagnostic never fires either — **the player is silently dead in a case
where HEAD keeps working.** On fire, the watchdog re-enters `handshake()` against whatever document
is current; if the webview has no live document, it emits the C6 diagnostic. `dom-ready` clears it.

In-page navigation must **not** tear down — the JS world survives it.

**5.4 Arming.** `dom-ready` clears `armWatchdog`, resets `attempts`, arms the cover timer (§5.6), and
calls `handshake()` **unconditionally** — the `if (rpc)` term at `:176` is deleted. `safeInsertCSS()`
stays at `:258`; it is already correct.

**5.5 `handshake()`** — per-attempt token `${NONCE}:${seq}`, replacing the module-constant `NONCE`
(`:56`) as the gate. Entry check first, then the numbered sequence:

```
if (!wv || rpc) return                       // rpc non-null here == acked for THIS document
if (attempts++ >= handshakeConfig.maxAttempts) return void onHandshakeFailed()
const seq = ++handshakeSeq
```

1. `await safeExec(guestRuntime(token))`. If `seq !== handshakeSeq` → **abandon silently** (a newer
   attempt or a teardown owns the world now).
2. Build `MessageChannel`; `const candidate = createRpc(port1)`; assign `pendingRpc = candidate`,
   **not** `rpc`. Immediately re-check `seq !== handshakeSeq` → destroy `candidate`, clear
   `pendingRpc` if it is still `candidate`, abandon (closes the teardown-between-1-and-2 window).
3. Transfer: `wv.contentWindow.postMessage(token, '*', [port2])`, **inside a `try/catch`** — HEAD
   wraps this at `:191-195` because `contentWindow` throws when null, and `handshake()` is invoked as
   `void handshake()`, so an uncaught throw becomes an unhandled rejection. On throw: destroy
   `candidate`, clear `pendingRpc`, `retryLater()`.
4. `const acked = await Promise.race([candidate.whenAck(token), delay(ackTimeoutMs) → false])` — and
   **use the result**, unlike `:197`, which discards it.
5. Resolve, splitting the two conditions that rev 1 wrongly merged:
   - `seq !== handshakeSeq` → destroy `candidate`, clear `pendingRpc`, **abandon — do not retry.**
     Retrying a superseded attempt re-arms the guest with a new token, and §6.4's `initPort` destroys
     the prior guest rpc — which would kill the guest end of a channel the host has already
     published, leaving `rpc` non-null with a dead peer. Exactly what C1 exists to prevent.
   - `!acked && seq === handshakeSeq` → destroy `candidate`, clear `pendingRpc`, `retryLater()`
     (backoff; bounded by the entry check).
6. Publish: `pendingRpc = null`, `rpc = candidate`, attach the `state`/`time` listeners.
   **Do not touch the cover here** (N5, C3).

`onHandshakeFailed()` emits exactly one `console.warn` carrying `webview.getURL()` (C6).

**Bounded per document, not globally.** `attempts` resets on each `dom-ready` and in `teardown()` —
consistent, both meaning "a new document" — so a redirect chain gets `maxAttempts` **per document**,
which is the correct semantics. There is no global circuit breaker; a pathologically self-redirecting
page yields repeated injections. Accepted, and stated so a future reader does not mistake it for an
oversight.

**5.6 Cover drop — decoupled from the handshake entirely.** `dom-ready` arms `coverTimer` for
`readyTimeoutMs` (10s, matching HEAD). The cover drops on **whichever comes first**:

- the guest's `ready` event (C3 — a `<video>` was hooked), or
- `coverTimer` firing, **regardless of handshake outcome**.

This preserves N5 (never drops on `ack`, so no early reveal) and N6 (a consent wall still reveals
after 10s, because `ready` never fires there and the handshake *succeeds*, so a failure-only branch
would leave the cover up permanently — the regression rev 1 shipped). `refreshSpinner()` is called on
every drop, as today.

**5.7 `load(id)`** calls `teardown()` in place of its inline `rpc?.destroy(); rpc = null`, then raises
the cover and reassigns `src` as today.

**5.8 `destroyPlayer()` must call `teardown()`.** At HEAD it does `rpc?.destroy(); rpc = null`
(`:396-397`) and nothing else; after §5.1 that would leave `pendingRpc` live, `handshakeSeq`/
`attempts` stale, and an in-flight `handshake()` still awaiting its ack delay. `playerSingleton.test.ts:39-42`
calls it in `afterEach`, so a leaked port plus a live 3s timer crossing a test boundary is a flake
generator. It must also clear `armWatchdog` and `coverTimer`.

**5.9 `unmount()`'s pause.** `:296` `void rpc?.invoke('pause')` becomes a no-op whenever `rpc` is null
— now a real window (up to `maxAttempts × ackTimeoutMs`). Closing the pane mid-handshake would leave
**audio playing**, which HEAD does not do. Fall back to `safeExec` with the guest's pause snippet when
`rpc` is null.

## 6. Guest-side design (`inject/youtube-guest.ts`)

**6.1 Sentinel-guard the IIFE (C5).** If `window.__linsaeGuest` exists, call its `arm(token)` and
return. Without this, a second injection installs a second copy of: 11 media-event listeners
(`:131-140`), a capture-phase `keydown` stopper (`:208`), two `MutationObserver`s (`:174-183`,
`:222-223`), **three** 200ms `setInterval`s (`:241-244`, `:263-266`, `:273-275`) and a second
`requestAnimationFrame` loop via `startRaf` (`:96-110`). `observerActive` (`:90`) guards only within
one closure and does nothing across two injected copies.

**6.2 Make the receiver re-armable (C5).** `:280-284` currently calls `removeEventListener` on first
match, so a second port transfer into the same document is silently dropped. Keep the listener
installed for the document lifetime; re-arm the expected token instead. Validate `e.ports.length === 1`.

**Do NOT add Electron's `event.source === window` check.** Its recipe assumes the message originates
from a preload in the same window; ours originates from the *host* via `contentWindow.postMessage`,
so `e.source` will not be `window`. What it actually is under Electron 42's inner-WebContents model
is **unverified** — copying the check could break the receiver outright.

**6.3 Send `ack(token)` as the first act of `initPort`** (C3), before any DOM work. `rpc.ready()` stays
in `attachVideo()` (`:155`) and now means only "video found".

**6.4 `initPort` destroys any prior `rpc`** before building the new one.

**6.5 Wire format — and the parity test that rev 1 lacked.** `rpc.ts`'s `Wire` union (`:6-10`) gains
`{ t: 'ack'; token: string }`, and the guest's inline `buildRpc` (`:24-70`) must match. These two wire
formats are duplicated by design (spec §D9) and **drift between them is silent** — an unknown `t`
falls off the end of `createRpc`'s `else if` chain (`rpc.ts:69-71`) and is dropped without error.
§8.2 T9 pins them against each other; that is the whole reason T9 exists in its new form.

**6.6 The ack surface on `Rpc` (rev 1 left `waitForAck` undefined).** The `Rpc` interface
(`rpc.ts:12-20`) has no ack member, and `createRpc` owns `port.onmessage` exclusively (`:45-72`), so a
caller cannot observe the port itself. Add:

```ts
whenAck(token: string): Promise<boolean>   // resolves true iff an {t:'ack'} echoing `token` arrives
```

Implemented inside `createRpc` alongside the existing `readyPromise` machinery (`:35-42`). It never
rejects and never times out on its own — §5.5 step 4 owns the timeout.

## 7. #211 — the cache half

**L1. Reset on every guest reset, not just `load()`** — `resetCache()` inside `teardown()` (§5.2),
covering the paths that bypass `load()` (consent redirect, YouTube self-reload). Fixes the stale
duration, the misscaled scrubber, and the `cache.last` suppression together.

**L2. Delete the `durationDone` latch** — declaration at `:32` **and** the polling block
(`usePlayer.ts:38-45`, `usePlayerState.ts:40-47`) in both files. Do not reset it: `setDuration(d)`
with an unchanged `d` is a React bail-out (`Object.is`), so the latch saves nothing and costs a
permanent-staleness failure mode. Keep the `d != null` check.

Two comment consequences, both real:
- `usePlayerState.ts:26`'s `biome-ignore useExhaustiveDependencies` justifies the `videoId` dep with
  *"dep ensures durationDone resets on video change"*. Deleting the latch invalidates the stated
  reason. **Keep the dep** (it is still a legitimate re-run trigger) and rewrite the justification.
- **`usePlayer.ts` has no such comment** — rev 1's "both files' comments" was wrong. Its
  `[videoId, hostRef]` deps are genuinely read in the body.
- `PlayerPane.tsx:118` cross-references `usePlayerState.ts:26` by line ("Same pattern as
  usePlayerState.ts:26"). Editing that comment drifts this reference — the exact class as
  #206/#209/#215. Update both together.

**L3 — OUT OF SCOPE, file as its own issue.** Stamping guest `state`/`time` payloads with the guest's
own `video_id` and emitting `navigate` from `yt-navigate-finish`. L1+L2 fix every path that goes
through a document load; **L3 closes the pure-SPA path**. The issue must say so explicitly.

**#211 is still truthfully closable with L1+L2.** Its acceptance is *"Opening video B after video A
persists B's own `durationSec`, and a test pins it"* — a `load()`-path claim L1 closes at the source.
Its item-3 `cache.last` concern is also closed: the guest's `flags()` hard-codes `ready: true`
(`youtube-guest.ts:75`), so `deriveState` (`player-state.ts:10-17`) can never return `'unstarted'`
from a guest event, and the reset value can never collide with the incoming video's first state.

**Ordering invariant the write-back depends on — stated because it is load-bearing and untested.**
`ThreadView.tsx:241-249`'s `durationWrittenRef` latches on the first non-null duration and never
rewrites, so the disk-corruption path is closed only if `usePlayer`'s effect (`player.load(B)` →
`teardown()` → cache reset) runs **before** `usePlayerState`'s first rAF tick reads `getDuration()`.
That holds because both are `useEffect`s flushed in the same commit before the next frame. §8.2 T10
pins it; the singleton-level T7 cannot see it.

## 8. Tests — every one mutation-verified

**Mandatory, from v0.8.2's lesson:** for each test, mutate the implementation, confirm it goes **red
for the right reason**, restore, and **record the mutation used in the task's report**. Four
assertions last milestone looked like proof and could not fail.

**8.1 The stub must be a fake guest, not a port capture.** `stubWebview` (`playerSingleton.test.ts:4-23`)
provides `executeJavaScript`, `insertCSS`, `setUserAgent` — **no `contentWindow`** — and nothing
anywhere dispatches `dom-ready`, so **every line of `playerSingleton.ts:174-205` is currently
unexecuted by Vitest**.

Capturing the transferred port is not enough: T1, T2, T5, T6 and T7 all need a guest that **acks** and
can **emit `state`/`time` to seed the cache**. Build `fakeGuest(port, opts)`:

```ts
// happy-dom has real MessageChannel/MessagePort — proven by rpc.test.ts:7-13.
function fakeGuest(port: MessagePort, o: { ack?: string | false; } = {}) {
  port.start()
  if (o.ack !== false) port.postMessage({ t: 'ack', token: o.ack })   // wrong/absent token = the T5 case
  return {
    emitState: (f) => port.postMessage({ t: 'event', event: 'state', payload: f }),
    emitReady: () => port.postMessage({ t: 'ready' }),
  }
}
```

**Observing `rpc`'s null-ness.** `rpc` is module-private and unexported; do **not** export it for
tests. The public proxy is: `player.pause()` posts an `invoke` on the live port when `rpc` is
non-null, and posts nothing when null. Assert on messages received at the fake guest.

Dispatch events as `Object.assign(new Event('dom-ready'), props)` — exactly how Electron constructs
them (`web-view-impl.ts:117-120`).

**8.3 Real-Electron gate — via `src` reassignment, not session interception.** Rev 1 specified
intercepting the first `/watch` request and serving an interstitial. That is **not implementable as
described**: `webRequest.onBeforeRequest`'s response accepts only `cancel` and `redirectURL` — no
body (`docs/api/web-request.md` @ v42.5.0); `onHeadersReceived` offers only `cancel`/
`responseHeaders`/`statusLine`; redirecting to `data:`/`file:` is blocked by Chromium for top-frame
navigations; a custom scheme needs `registerSchemesAsPrivileged` before `app.ready`, which the smoke
cannot reach through `electronApp.evaluate`; and whether `ses.protocol.handle('https', …)` can
intercept `https` on a `persist:` partition is undocumented and unverified.

**Instead, reproduce the wide window with no new Electron API at all.** The smoke already reaches the
element (`scripts/thread-smoke.mjs:778-786` does `win.evaluate(… querySelector('#yt-player-wrapper webview'))`
and calls `executeJavaScript` on it). Under `SMOKE_FORCE_SWAP=1`:

1. Set `wv.src = 'https://www.youtube.com/'` **directly**, bypassing `load()` so nothing clears `rpc`.
2. Wait for the guest runtime to be present on that document.
3. Set `wv.src` to the watch URL.

On HEAD, `rpc` latches to the homepage document and the watch page's `dom-ready` is refused by `:176`
→ the host never receives a duration → the `walled` predicate at `scripts/thread-smoke.mjs:847`
(`!diag || diag.consent || !diag.hasVideo || !diag.href?.includes('/watch')`) is **false** → the gate
**fails** rather than skips, exactly as its authors designed. After the fix it passes. The homepage is
literally what the consent wall redirects to (`playerSingleton.ts:9-10`), so this is the real shape.

**Acceptance: fails 100% of runs on HEAD, passes 100% after — 3 consecutive runs each way.** If it
does **not** fail on HEAD, the wide-window diagnosis is wrong and the narrow race is the real
mechanism — **report that rather than adjusting the repro until it reproduces.**

**8.4 Smoke consent readout.** `scripts/thread-smoke.mjs:837-878` infers "port dead vs consent wall"
